Sheet 1 of 1

Draft 3 · North star

One pipe,
where a lattice should be. A working survey of why the game is inert, and the shape of the one that isn't.

The engine is sound. The problem is that everything in this game flows down a single pipe — one worker, making one number — and every system built since has only changed how fast.

01

The question isn't why an idle game is boring

Melvor Idle is timers, and holds players for hundreds of hours. Kittens Game is resources accumulating. CIFI took the mobile category award three years running selling upgrade trees. None of them has moment-to-moment decisions either.

So the honest question is why this one is inert when those are not — and the answer is structural, visible in the code, and fixable without leaving the genre.

Now A single pipe

Three separate attempts at a build decision each moved single digits — the clause pool, Tier III licences, the Commendation repair. They were all multipliers on the same pipe.

Next A lattice

Several producers, several chains, each with its own number and its own bottleneck. Allocation is the one reward shape that cannot collapse into a multiplier — which is why "concurrent postings" kept surfacing in the roadmap and kept being deferred.

02

Three measurements, already in the repository

None of this is speculation. balance.md and longrun have been reporting it for months — the findings were read as tuning problems because there was no frame that made them one story.

03

The genre runs on five decisions

This is the whole diagnosis on one sheet. Every game in the competitive sample offers most of these. We offer about half of one.

04

Seven changes, in order of how much they matter

Stop selling multipliers on one producer. Build several producers, several chains, and make the player allocate between them.

CHANGE 00

Put an orb on the screen and mine it

The game is currently screens of text. (the) Gnorp Apologue is a rock in the middle of the screen that little creatures hit until shards fly off, and it is beloved for exactly that — the design write-up is titled "You have to hit the rock."

So: one object, centre screen, always visible, visibly changing. Cracked, pitted, shedding, worked. It does three jobs at once.

It unifies the escalation. A working, a district, a world, a star — every rung of the ladder is an orb, and enclosing a star is building an orb around an orb. One renderer covers hour one and the endgame (Figure 7).

It makes upgrades behavioural. Gnorp's developer names the mechanism exactly: "with individually simulated units, it allows you to create interesting upgrades beyond a simple percentage increase to their output." When a recruit is a row in a table, an upgrade can only scale it.

It gives the fiction something to be about. An orb invites orb worshippers — and under the Authority, a theological objection is a form. Received, logged, overruled on a schedule. Free content axis, free restrictions for the challenge catalogue, funny at no art cost.

AND STEAL THEIR BEST MECHANIC — THE ORB RECLAIMS.
Unprocessed output gets absorbed back, faster the more of it there is. A bottleneck that moves, punishes hoarding, and cannot be solved by accumulating — only by throughput. Gold has bound this game for an entire career; this is the second constraint it has never had.

CHANGE 01

A roster, not a recruit

Several recruits working in parallel, each with their own aptitudes, each assigned to a working and a depth. This is the single change that creates decisions without needing a writer — and roster size and recruit quality are both numbers to buy.

Darkest Dungeon keeps a roster of 20+ so no death cripples the campaign. Majesty directs autonomous heroes with bounties rather than orders. Tap Titans 2 runs a hero roster. All three point the same way.

CHANGE 02

Chains that feed chains

Today it is delve → gold, and the market is a place to turn that output into the same number. Instead: raw material feeds refining, refining feeds equipment, equipment feeds deeper delving, depth feeds rarer material. Each link has its own number and its own bottleneck.

Kittens Game's storage caps, taken literally: repeatedly being unable to afford a goal because you cannot hold enough turns waiting into planning. Planning is a decision.

CHANGE 03

Prestige becomes the loop, and you time it

Form R-1 promoted from a form to the progression path — this is Discharge, rung one. Death becomes a failure mode of pushing too hard, not the payout mechanism, so depth is a real risk instead of the only route forward.

And you choose what carries over. Sunless Sea offers five inheritances at a captain's death; the Correspondent hands over the entire discovered chart. That is a prestige decision rather than a payout, and the cheapest source of build divergence available to us.

This also closes the worst bug in the project's history: when the HP curve outran damage, recruits stopped dying — and because death was the only pension source, progression stopped dead on day 14 and never resumed. A prestige loop a balance error can switch off is not a loop.

CHANGE 04

Unlocks must be verbs

Retire the nineteen rate multipliers, or demote them to a cheap background tier bought without thinking. Meta-currency should buy new systems: a second recruit slot, a new chain, a working with its own rules, a piece of automation, a challenge that replays content you've beaten with one rule changed.

The test, borrowed from our own tuning comment about the permit that granted nothing: if a rung cannot be described without the words “faster”, “more” or “cheaper”, it is not a rung.

CHANGE 05

Familiarity — mastery in the register

Knowledge should be what survives a recruit — and the genre already has that idea as a number. Melvor's Mastery accrues per activity with use, plus a pool that accelerates the rest, with thresholds that unlock capability.

So: Familiarity, accrued per working, held by the office rather than the recruit. It survives every discharge, and it unlocks options at thresholds rather than only rates. Number-go-up, genre-native, and it does the job the pension was failing to do.

Melvor rebuilt Mastery entirely after launch because the first version's sense of progression was sub-par. Worth knowing this system is hard to get right — and survivable to redo.

CHANGE 06

Resets that reset the resets

One reset loop runs out — that isn't a prediction, it's Figure 3. Every game that lasts for months answers it the same way: resets that reset the resets. But the ones that do it well share a property that is easy to miss, and it is the whole point:

EACH LAYER IS NOT A BIGGER VERSION OF THE ONE BELOW.
IT IS A DIFFERENT MACHINE — ITS OWN CURRENCY, ITS OWN UPGRADE MECHANIC, ITS OWN SCREEN.

Antimatter Dimensions is the clearest case. Infinity gives you a flat purchase grid. Eternity replaces it with a branching tree whose branches are mutually exclusive. Reality replaces that with equippable, procedurally rolled glyphs, a perk web, and finally a scripting language for automating everything beneath it. Four layers, four genuinely different interfaces — not four multiplier columns.

Every rung reaches downward into the layers below and also feeds itself. That self-feeding term is what makes a ladder accelerate rather than merely stack, and it is what a flat "prestige 2 grants +200%" never has.

And each rung zooms out. One shaft, then a career, then a district, a world, a system — and finally a star, enclosed shell by shell. Universal Paperclips starts on a clicker and ends disassembling the universe; Kittens Game ends in orbit. Scope escalation is the genre's signature move and this design was stopping at "a slightly bigger mine".

THE JOKE IS THAT THE PAPERWORK NEVER CHANGES.
YOU FILE THE SAME FORM FOR A STELLAR ENCLOSURE THAT YOU FILED FOR ONE RECRUIT'S BOOT ALLOWANCE.

And the part that makes it affordable: each reset generates the next board from a new seed. Because nothing in this game is drawn by hand (§08), a new holding — or a new posting — costs no asset, no map and no artist. The prestige ladder and the generated-art constraint turn out to be the same decision seen from two sides, and together they are the answer to Figure 3.

Which costume, then

Flipping Figure 4 between the two makes the real point: the systems don't care. But the costumes are not equally good, and the bureaucratic one turns out to fit this ladder better than the design that replaced it. Both cases, honestly.

KEEP THE AUTHORITY

  • The clause system already exists. Eighty clauses across six dimensions, endorsements and riders, and Form 12-C rerolls one. That is a glyph system — the hardest rung on the ladder — and it shipped.
  • Prestige is promotion. Every rung is being moved away from the job you had just got good at. That is funny, true, and nobody else in the genre has it.
  • Tone is a moat. Our own research: NGU's second most-praised quality after progression is the writing, and it is "the one thing a better-funded competitor cannot copy convincingly."
  • The shelf is crowded with the alternative. CIFI is a cell factory, USI is space, Melvor is fantasy skills. A deadpan bureaucracy is the only one of these that is distinctive in a store listing.
  • Thousands of lines of written content survive rather than being deleted — the glossary, the flavour corpus, the register rule.
  • The joke wore out because it had nothing to be about. It was 528 lines of glossary explaining a game with one pipe. Give it five real machines and the paperwork has something to be absurd against — which is what flavor.ts already says: "the form is funny because the corridor is real."

GO TO THE HOLDINGS

  • It is legible in one screen. Seams, depth, yield, refining — no glossary required. Competitors lose most churned players in the first thirty minutes, to the interface rather than the game.
  • You said the joke wore out, and that is data. A theme its owner is sick of is a theme that will not get written well for another year.
  • Big numbers sit naturally in an industrial frame. A bureau reporting 4.71e12 is a tonal choice you have to keep making work.
  • Procedural art is native — contours, seams, surveys. Documents are drawable too, but terrain is the easier generative brief.
  • The risk it removes: a bureaucratic theme invites more vocabulary rather than more systems. That is precisely what happened — each new form was a new multiplier with a joke attached.

The recommendation

Keep the Authority, and hold it to the same rule as the unlocks. The bureaucracy failed as decoration on a dead system; it is a genuinely strong frame for five interlocking machines, and the clause rung is already built.

The condition is the part that matters: every piece of vocabulary must be attached to a mechanic. No glossary entry without a system behind it, no form that is not a screen you use. If a term cannot name something the player does, it gets cut — the same test that condemned nineteen unlocks, pointed at the writing.

That is "keep the frame, cut the paperwork" taken seriously, and it is a smaller change than the Holdings rewrite while keeping the one asset a better-funded competitor cannot copy.

06

This is a spine transplant, not a rewrite

An earlier draft deleted 12–14k lines, pointed at the wrong genre, and was about to throw away the three most genre-correct things in the codebase — the automation ladder, the challenge catalogue, and the clause system that turns out to be an entire prestige rung. Roughly 4–6k lines change.

KEEP and make load-bearing

  • Lazy resolution & the tick watermark. Nothing runs per player between requests. This is what makes offline progression real, and it is genuinely good work.
  • Deterministic seeding on (character, tick). The same span resolves the same way twice — which is what lets the tests assert on it.
  • The simulation harness. simulate, longrun, forecast — the most valuable asset here. It needs new questions, not new code.
  • staff.ts — an automation ladder where every hire takes a standing instruction rather than a toggle. "Automation that removes the decision removes the game." Exactly right.
  • assignments.ts — Antimatter Dimensions-style challenges. Best content-per-authored-line mechanic in the genre.
  • Clauses, endorsements and riders. Eighty across six dimensions, with Form 12-C to reroll one and Form 19 to move one. That is the Commission rung's glyph system, already built — the hardest rung on the ladder, and the previous draft was deleting it.
  • Storage ports, migrations, auth, rate limiting, idempotency, health. All of it.

CUT or rebuild

  • The 19 pension unlocks — rebuilt as verbs; rate tiers demoted to background.
  • Permits and grade as depth gates — eight rungs saying the same sentence.
  • Death as the prestige trigger — decoupled; R-1 promoted in its place.
  • resolve.ts rules — rebuilt for a roster. The structure stays.
  • Case files, licences, filings, clearances — folded into Familiarity and the lattice, or cut. Clauses are not in this list — see opposite.
  • Vocabulary that names nothing. The institution stays and the register stays, but every term must name a mechanic. A glossary entry with no system behind it is cut — the unlock test, aimed at the writing.
07

One week that can kill it

Stage 0 · headless · no UI

Three recruits, two workings, one extra chain. Run the allocation-spread test: do simulated players who allocate differently end the week materially apart?

If allocation converges on one best assignment, stop. The thesis is wrong, everything downstream of it is decoration, and it cost a week rather than a quarter. This is the same instrument that condemned the retreat slider — pointed at the mechanic before it ships instead of a year after.

08

Nothing here gets drawn by hand

There is no artist on this project, so the visual language has to be something a program can produce — and the architecture already hands us that for free.

Every roll in this game is keyed on (character, tick) through tickSeed. The same span resolves the same way twice. That means a seam can render as a pure function of the seed that generated it — the picture and the simulation are the same object, and a site always looks like itself.

This is why the direction is cartographic rather than illustrative. Orbs, contours, seam lattices, node graphs, plotted survey sheets: all of it is arithmetic and two colours of ink. No character art, no item icons, no sprite sheet, nothing that needs a hand.

Gnorp's other free trick, worth taking whole: it ships "a mostly monochromatic colour scheme so that players would feel they were bringing colour into the world as they progressed." The orb starts dead and gains colour as it is worked — progress made legible without a single drawn asset.

09

Decisions taken, and the one still open

How big do the numbers get Full incremental scale Wherever the maths goes, scientific notation included. A lattice produces large numbers arithmetically; refusing them means capping the lattice. "Gold in the hundreds" cannot survive Change 02, and shouldn't be defended.
What is the fiction Keep the Authority — recommended, your call The ladder settles an argument this document previously got backwards. Prestige reads as promotion, the clause rung is already built, and paperwork that never changes scale is a joke that improves as the objects get absurd. The condition: every term must name a mechanic.
What survives a recruit Familiarity, and whatever you chose Not a pension. Per-working knowledge held by the office, plus the inheritance you pick at discharge. The office remembers; the recruit does not.
Where the ladder stops Six rungs designed, five to build Enclosure is the designed terminal and the seventh rung is deliberately unbuilt. A rung that cannot buy a different kind of thing than the rung below it is not a rung — that test decides whether there is ever a seventh.
How the art gets made Generated, never drawn No artist on the project, so no asset pipeline that needs one. Seams, contours, roster diagrams and charts are all functions of the seed the resolver already computes. The cartographic direction was chosen because it is the one that a program can draw convincingly.
What you are looking at An orb, at every scale One object centre screen, mined visibly. The same renderer draws the working, the world and the enclosure, which is the only reason a project with no artist can promise a stellar endgame. It reclaims what you fail to process, and people worship it.
Still open How the roster is directed Standing instructions in the staff.ts model — delegate with a policy — or Majesty-style bounties, where autonomous people take the work that pays best. The second is more interesting and considerably less proven. Stage 0 can answer it.
